# SIM-518 — fit and turn on the pitcher-fatigue factor: the build plan (2026-09-12, revised the same day on three owner rulings)

**Owner summary.** The simulator picks each pitch by drawing one real pitch from a pool of
past pitches, weighted by how much the live situation resembles each row's. On 2026-09-07
the team added three facts the draw could not see before: how tired the pitcher is (his
pitch count and how many times he has faced the order), which side is batting (home or
away), and, for a ball in play, how similar the pitch that produced each candidate batted
ball is to the pitch just thrown. The data for all three is in the pool and in the
artifact bundle today.

**Three owner rulings on 2026-09-12 narrow the ticket to one factor and change how it is
judged:**

1. **The grade is the sim-vs-closing-line accuracy comparison** (SIM-538): the
   simulator's probability and the market's closing probability, both scored against what
   happened in the real game, paired per game. The pool-totals bands (the 45-game lane)
   no longer validate the model.
2. **The pitch-similarity factor on the fielding draw is dropped.** Since the play-picker
   redesign the fielding draw conditions on the actual batted ball the drawn pitch
   produced, so the pitch reaches the fielding draw through the ball. The bandwidth stays
   at zero; the code stays, byte-identical off and tested.
3. **The batting side is already a hard filter**, as it should be: since 2026-09-08 the
   pitch draw runs inside a hard-filter cell and the side is one of its five dimensions.
   Nothing to fit.

What remains is **the pitcher-fatigue factor**: fit its strength, measure what it does to
the predictions, and judge it with a paired accuracy run. The answer to the owner's
question — "do we need a baseline run to know whether fatigue helps?" — is **yes, as the
OFF arm of a paired run, not as a standalone baseline**, and §6 says why and what it can
and cannot resolve.

No data rebuild is needed. Every step is read-only on the database, so the DuckDB lock
(SIM-524) does not block it.

**Read first:** `docs/audit/2026-09-04-sim467-518-plan.md` (the epic's original plan; §6
and §8 are stamped BUILT), `docs/audit/2026-09-08-sim523-play-picker-redesign-plan.md`

## 1. The rules this plan works under (do not relitigate)

1. **The architecture rule, both clauses.** Every decision is a similarity-weighted draw
   from a hard-filtered pool, never a formula. The drawn row is the play. Every factor is
   a draw weight or OFF until its weight lands. The pitch draw picks from the live
   count's own list of candidate rows; a weight re-ranks the rows inside that list and
   changes nothing about which list is used. The mean-of-1 rescaling inside each list is
   housekeeping — it changes no probability; it gives a row with no value the average
   weight (the fielder-kernel lesson of 2026-08-30) and keeps the numbers well-scaled.
2. **Two instruments, two jobs.** The pool's own conditional SETS a bandwidth: a factor is
   right when the sim's frequencies, conditioned on the fact the factor reads, match the
   pool's own frequencies conditioned on the same fact. That fit runs on millions of
   plate appearances and resolves in minutes. The accuracy comparison JUDGES the model:
   whether the probabilities it serves are closer to what happened than the market's, and
   whether a change made them better or worse. Do not fit a bandwidth on one season's
   outcomes (it overfits that season, and §6 shows it lacks the power anyway).
3. **A paired read or no read.** Any ON-versus-OFF claim under the accuracy metric comes
   from the same games, the same closing lines, the same real outcomes and the same
   seeds, scored as a per-game difference with a game-clustered confidence range, on a
   frozen bundle. A number without its range is not a result (SIM-539).
4. **Measure before building.** Every step starts with a number the pool alone can give
   before any game is simulated.

## 5. The fatigue factor — the fit

**What the factor does.** For the live pitcher's pitch count before the plate appearance
(`c`) and his times through the order (`t`), each candidate row in the live count
sub-cell is weighted by a Gaussian on |c − row pitch count| times a Gaussian on |t − row
TTO|, then rescaled so the list's average weight is 1 (housekeeping; no probability
changes). The factor re-ranks the rows inside the live count's list — every row from
every pitcher in the cell, not the live pitcher's own rows — and nothing else: which
count the game is in comes from the previous pitch's result. The pool's own outcome mix
at that fatigue state then decides the pitch.

**The trap, stated once.** In the pool, rows at pitch count 90+ come from pitchers who
were allowed to reach 90 — better pitchers, or pitchers cruising that day — and rows at
pitch count 0-15 come mostly from relievers, who throw harder and strike out more. A
kernel on the pitch count therefore carries a PITCHER-TYPE signal on top of the fatigue
signal, and the flat pitcher identity factor cannot offset it. The
times-through-the-order term carries less of this (a starter's third time through is a
starter's, whoever he is), and it is the effect the literature documents most firmly.
The fit separates the two signals and puts the size of the imported one in front of the
owner.

### 5.1 Step 1 — the offline scan (the pool alone; `scripts/sim518_fatigue_scan.py`; minutes to run)

No game is simulated: the reference and the kernel are functions of the same pool
columns. Per hand, per count sub-cell (the cell index's own grouping, widened exactly as
the sampler widens):

1. **The pool's own conditional (the reference).** The outcome mix (ball, called strike,
   swinging strike, foul, in play, hit by pitch) by pitch-count band (0-25 / 26-50 /
   51-75 / 76-100 / 100+) and by TTO (1 / 2 / 3 / 4+), recency-weighted, in two forms:
   * *Marginal:* the raw mix per band.
   * *Within-pitcher:* for each pitcher-season with rows in at least two bands, his
     per-band mix minus his own overall mix, pooled across pitchers weighted by his rows.
     This is the fatigue effect with the "who pitches deep" selection removed.
2. **The kernel's expected conditional** for a ladder of bandwidth pairs: for a live
   state (c, t), the kernel-weighted mix over the sub-cell's rows, at the band midpoints
   and TTO 1-4. Ladder: TTO sigma ∈ {off, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}; pitch-count sigma ∈ {off, 10,
   20, 35, 60}. Read per arm:
   * the **spread ratio** — the kernel's top-band-minus-bottom-band spread over the
     pool's own, for the ball share, the whiff share, the called-strike share and the
     in-play share, against BOTH references;
   * the **effective sample share** of the sub-cell the kernel keeps (100% = flat);
   * the **survivorship read** — the kernel-weighted mean of the ROW pitcher's own
     season strikeout rate and walk rate by live band, against the sub-cell's unweighted
     mean, in points of rate per plate appearance. A kernel that raises row-pitcher
     quality at high live counts imports selection.

**The fit rule.** The candidate is the largest (softest) pair whose expected
within-pitcher spread ratio sits within ±20% of 1.0 for the ball and whiff shares, with
the survivorship read under the ceiling the owner sets at decision point A (proposed: no
more than 0.5 points of strikeout rate at any live band — about a quarter of the pool's
own between-band spread). If no pair on the ladder clears both, the TTO-only arm
(pitch-count sigma off) is the fallback candidate.

**STEP 1 RAN 2026-09-13** (`scripts/sim518_fatigue_scan.py`, 2,771,222 rows, 3,402
pitcher-seasons, 1,947 with rows in two or more bands; `scripts/sim518_fatigue_scan.json`).
The findings, in points of share (×100):

* **The within-pitcher fatigue effect (B) is real and small.** By times through the order
  (1st → 3rd): called strikes +0.74 → −1.09, whiffs +0.20 → −0.43, balls −0.09 → +0.46,
  balls in play −0.67 → +0.87. By pitch-count band (0-25 → 76-100): called +0.49 → −1.24,
  whiff +0.09 → −0.12, ball +0.13 → −0.09, in play −0.58 → +0.91. The marginal (A) differs
  from B mainly at 100+ pitches (0.12% of the mass) and on the whiff channel by TTO
  (A −0.68 vs B −0.43 at the 3rd): the selection part is about a quarter of the whiff
  spread and none of the called-strike spread.
* **The pitch-count term earns nothing the TTO term does not, and it imports selection.**
  Every pitch-count arm moves the ball share the WRONG way at the third time through
  (−0.15 to −0.30 against B's +0.46) — "pitchers allowed to pitch deep throw fewer balls".
  The TTO-only arms reach the same called-strike and in-play fit.
* **What any kernel does most is fix a ROLE mismatch, not import quality.** At the
  76-100-pitch live band the flat draw's rows are 57.6% starters' — a starter deep in his
  start draws 42% reliever pitches. Every kernel takes the starter share to ~98%. WITHIN
  starters, the kernel raises the drawn rows' pitcher whiff share by 0.04-0.10 points (TTO
  only) or 0.12-0.19 (pitch count) — far under the 0.5-point ceiling. The mirror cost: at
  0-25 pitches the TTO kernel LOWERS the starter share (first-time-through rows are
  reliever-heavy); §5.2 sizes it in the loop.
* **The candidate:** TTO only, σ = 0.5 — called −0.99 vs B −1.09, whiff −0.66 vs −0.43,
  in play +1.25 vs +0.87, ball +0.13 vs +0.46; 59% of each sub-cell kept. σ = 0.7 is the
  softer alternative (~75% of the effect, 74% kept). The ball rise within pitcher is not
  reproducible by any kernel here (the marginal carries less of it than B), which is the
  selection working against the fit, not a bandwidth question.

### 5.2 Step 2 — the in-loop probe and the prediction-shift read

**Cost correction.** The original estimate (300 games × 200 iterations × 3 arms ≈ 2.5 h) was
wrong by an order of magnitude: that is 180,000 game-sims, about 43 hours serial. The probe
runs on the balanced 45 games × 40 iterations per arm, three arms as parallel processes
(`scripts/sim518_fatigue_probe.py run --arm pc:tto`; `report` pairs them), about 35 minutes
wall. The within-pitcher read resolves a one-point shift at that size; the per-starter
prediction shift averages over 90 starter-games with a standard error near 0.01.

The loop dilutes any factor (the situation kernel, recency and the batter factor act on
the same rows), so the offline candidate is confirmed in the loop, and the size of its
effect on the PREDICTION is measured before any accuracy run is scheduled. One probe
script, three arms (OFF, the candidate pair, the TTO-only fallback), on 300 games from
2024 with a listed starter on both sides, 200 iterations per arm, same seeds.

Per plate appearance the probe records the live pitcher, his (c, t) at the first pitch
and the terminal outcome; per game it records each starter's strikeout and earned-run
distributions. It prints:

* the sim's outcome mix by band and TTO, marginal and within-pitcher, against the pool's
  own — **the fit read**;
* the sim's plate-appearance share by band and TTO against the pool's row share — **the
  composition read**. If the sim spends 20% of its plate appearances at 76+ pitches
  where the pool spends 10%, the manager formula is the cause; the finding goes on the
  manager ticket (SIM-427), not on the kernel;
* the sim-side survivorship read (the drawn rows' pitcher quality by live band);
* **the prediction shift**: per starter-game, the ON arm's probability of clearing his
  strikeout line minus the OFF arm's, and the mean strikeouts ON minus OFF, stratified by
  how deep he went (under 75 pitches / 75-99 / 100+). This is the number §6's power
  arithmetic needs: the size of the change the accuracy comparison would have to see;
* the per-iteration wall time against the OFF arm (expect no measurable cost).

**Seed split.** Fit on odd seeds, confirm on even seeds.

**STEP 2 RAN 2026-09-13** (`scripts/sim518_fatigue_probe.py`; arms OFF / TTO σ 0.5 / TTO
σ 0.7 on the balanced 45 games × 40 iterations, 1.0 s per iteration, 31 minutes in parallel;
`scripts/sim518_probe_{off,tto05,tto07}.json`; the report is in `CHANGES.md`, 2026-09-13).

* **The fit read.** At the third time through, within pitcher, in points of share against the
  pool's own: σ 0.5 — called −1.10 (−1.09), whiff −0.51 (−0.43), in play +0.82 (+0.87),
  ball −0.01 (+0.46), foul +0.78 (+0.19); σ 0.7 — −0.95, −0.46, +1.05, +0.25, +0.12. By the
  rule (the largest bandwidth inside 0.25 points on every channel) **σ 0.7 is the fit**;
  σ 0.5 misses ball by 0.47 and foul by 0.59. The OFF arm has no third-time effect. The even
  and odd halves agree. Within-role selection at 76-100 pitches: +0.13 (whiff) / −0.15
  (ball) points — under the ceiling.
* **The composition read is the headline.** The sim spends 2.6% of pitches at 76-100 against
  the pool's 7.4%, 0.03% at 100+ against 0.12%, and 41% on the second time through against
  24%. Every starter throws 80-81 pitches (spread 1.6 across 90 starter-games; the majors
  ~87 ± 10) for 14.2 outs (the majors ~15.6), and 1.4 relievers a side then throw ~50 pitches
  each. This is the manager formula's pitch budget (SIM-434; the pitching-change draw is
  built and OFF — SIM-427). It caps what any fatigue factor can do: the deep states barely
  occur, and the sim's long relievers get matched to starters' second-time-through rows.
* **The prediction shift is nil.** Per starter-game: mean strikeouts −0.03 (σ 0.5) / +0.03
  (σ 0.7); the probability of clearing the closing strikeout line −0.003 ± 0.012 /
  +0.000 ± 0.010. The kernel moves a starter's strikeouts EARLIER (K/PA 22.8% the first time
  through against 21.5% OFF; 19.2% the third against 21.2%) and leaves his game total alone.
  Walks fall 1.5% (σ 0.7) to 2.7% (σ 0.5) relative — the matched rows at the second and
  third time through are starters', who walk fewer than the relievers the flat draw mixed in.
  §6's staging table puts this in the "under 0.01" row: a full-game prop's accuracy score
  cannot register it.

**THE RECOMMENDATION (2026-09-13).** The fitted value is **TTO-only, σ 0.7; the pitch-count
term OFF** (built, measured, imports selection on the ball channel). Whether to LAND it is
the owner's call between two honest positions:

* *Land it.* It reproduces the pool's own within-pitcher effect on every channel, its
  selection import is a tenth of the ceiling, and the third time through now looks like the
  third time through in the play-by-play. Its cost is a 1.5% relative walk drift and a
  starter share 12 points lower on his first 25 pitches (first-time-through rows are
  reliever-heavy). It moves no full-game prop probability, so the accuracy comparison
  cannot show it helping or hurting; the OFF arm of that run is still worth running as the

* *Hold it OFF until the manager model is fixed.* The kernel's main lever — matching a
  starter deep in his start to starters' rows — acts on states the sim rarely visits, and
  its early-game side effect acts on states the sim over-visits. Land the pitching-change
  draw (SIM-427) first, then re-run the probe on a realistic composition.

I recommend the second: the probe's largest finding is the composition, and a factor tuned
on a distorted composition is a fit to the wrong sim. The instrument set is ready to re-run
in 35 minutes once SIM-427 lands. Either way, the ticket's data half is done and the
composition numbers go on the manager ticket.


With steps 1 and 2 in hand, the owner sets the survivorship ceiling and, under it, the
pair. Three outcomes, each honest:

* **Both terms clear the ceiling** → the pair goes forward to §6.
* **Only the TTO term clears it** → TTO alone goes forward; the pitch-count term is
  recorded "built, measured, imports pitcher selection at every bandwidth on the ladder;
  OFF until the pitcher identity factor learns results".
* **Neither clears it** → both stay OFF, with the numbers; the ticket closes as
  measured-and-off, and §6 does not run for it.

If the owner would rather remove the selection than cap it, the build is a normalization
group of (sub-cell × row-pitcher role), so the kernel moves weight among starters' rows
and among relievers' rows but never between them. The pitch pool has no role column; the
pitcher profile has the starts share. That is a small sampler change plus a bundle
column, NOT in this plan's estimate — decision point C, taken only if the TTO-only arm
also fails.

## 6. The grade — the paired accuracy run (the owner's question, answered)

**Do we need a baseline?** Yes — as the OFF arm of one paired experiment. The OFF and ON
arms run on the same 2024 games, the same closing lines, the same real outcomes, the same
`--base-seed`, the same frozen bundle and calibration file, one after the other. The
read per market is the mean per-game difference (ON Brier − OFF Brier) with a
game-clustered bootstrap range, from the two reports' records paired on (`game_pk`,
market, `player_id`). A standalone baseline made earlier is reusable ONLY if nothing
changed in between; since the bundle was rebuilt on 2026-09-10 and the odds re-load is
still running, the two arms run together after the re-load.

**What the OFF arm is worth on its own.** It is the platform's first full-season read
under the metric the owner now trusts: the simulator against the closing line on every
market, with the SIM-539 ranges and minimum samples. Every later factor compares against
it. Keep its report as the reference.

**The power arithmetic (read this before scheduling).** Per record the Brier score is
(p − y)². The paired difference is (p_on − p_off) × (p_on + p_off − 2y), roughly
2 × (the change in probability) × (how wrong the forecast was). Its noise is almost all
Monte-Carlo: at 100 iterations each arm's probability carries a standard error near 0.05,
so the per-game difference has a noise band near ±0.07. Over about 4,000 starter K-prop
records (one season, two starters a game) the standard error of the mean difference is
about 0.001. A fatigue-sized improvement — a probability shift of ~0.02 on the third of
starts that go deep — is worth about 0.0003-0.0005 on that scale. **One season at 100
iterations cannot resolve a change of that size**; it CAN say "not worse than the OFF arm
within ±0.001" and give the direction. Five hundred iterations brings the range to about
±0.0005 (near the effect); a thousand resolves it, at ten times the machine time.

**So the run is staged by what §5.2's prediction-shift read says:**

| §5.2 prediction shift on deep starts | The §6 run |
|---|---|
| under 0.01 in probability | the full-season paired run at 100 iterations, all markets (~6-7 h an arm with `--workers 6`; the OFF arm is the reference either way). Expect "not resolvable"; record it as such. |
| 0.01-0.03 | the same, plus a **deep-start subset** at 500 iterations: the 2024 games where a starter with a K line threw 90+ pitches — roughly 1,000-1,500; the box score says which — both arms (~2 × 4-6 h). Stratify the read by pitch depth. |
| over 0.03 | the 100-iteration full run resolves it on its own. |

**The read.** Per market: N records, N games, the mean paired difference and its range,
the SIM-539 minimum, and — the fund's actual question — whether the ON arm is closer to
the outcomes than the market where the OFF arm was not. Report the strikeout market
first (the only 2024 pitcher market with lines besides earned runs), then earned runs,
then the game totals (fatigue moves late-inning runs; expect dilution), then everything
else as the "not worse" check. State the profile-bundle point-in-time gap on every read.

recommendation follows the read: land it if the strikeout market is not worse and the
direction is right; hold it if any market is worse outside its range.

## 10. Traps, pre-armed

* **Fatigue reads "before the plate appearance".** The pool column is pitches thrown
  before the PA; the loop passes `pitcher_pitch_count − 1` on the PA's first pitch. The
  scan buckets the pool by the same definition, or every band is off by one.
* **The composition read is not a fit target.** If the sim over-visits deep counts, the
  cure is the manager model, not a tighter or looser kernel.
* **The two arms must be twins.** Same bundle (manifest timestamp), same
  `calibration.json`, same `--base-seed`, same game list, same odds rows. The pairing
  script refuses anything else. No nightly artifact build between the arms.
* **The OFF arm and the odds re-load cannot overlap.** The re-load rewrites the odds
  rows the backtest reads; wait for the owner's word that it finished.
* **The point-in-time gap.** The pools stop at the day before each game; the profile
  bundle does not. State it on every read; it is the same for both arms, so it does not
  bias the paired difference.
* **Do not touch `sit_sigma`, the batter power or MIN_CELL** inside this ticket.
* **The image rebuild fills C:.** Prune first, build once. **Two heavy containers.** A
  backtest arm with six workers IS a heavy container; nothing else heavy beside it.
* **`scripts/` and `tests/` are not mounted.** `-v "$PWD/scripts:/app/scripts" -v
  "$PWD/tests:/app/tests"` on every run. `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` from Git Bash.
